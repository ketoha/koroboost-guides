Midnight Season 2 Gearing: How the New Item Level System Works

A guide by Koroboost

Season 2 brings a full item level reset, a currency rename, and some welcome changes to how bonus rolls and the Great Vault work. If you're trying to figure out what ilvl you should be targeting and how to get there, here's the complete breakdown.

Season 2 Item Level Table

Every gear track shifts up for the new season. Here's the full spread from base to fully upgraded:

| Track | Base ilvl | Max ilvl |

|-------|-----------|----------|

| Adventurer | 259 | 276 |

| Veteran | 272 | 289 |

| Champion | 285 | 302 |

| Hero | 298 | 315 |

| Myth | 311 | 328 |

The track system works the same as Season 1 -- you earn gear at a base ilvl, then upgrade it using crests. Each upgrade step adds a few ilvl points until you hit the track cap. The big difference is everything is shifted higher.

For endgame raiders and M+ pushers, the target is Myth track at 328. But there are ways to go even higher with special items (more on that below).

Worth noting: the gap between tracks is consistent at 13 ilvl base. This means jumping from Veteran to Champion gear is a noticeable power increase, not just a marginal stat bump. Prioritize your highest-impact slots (weapon, trinkets) when deciding where to spend your first upgrade crests.

Mistcrests: The New Currency

Flightstones are gone. Season 2 renames the upgrade currency to Mistcrests. Functionally they work the same -- you earn them from all content types and spend them alongside crests to upgrade gear. The rename fits the Midnight aesthetic, and the earn rates have been adjusted so early-season gearing feels a bit smoother.

You'll still need the right crest tier to upgrade within each track:

  • Weathered Crests for Adventurer/Veteran upgrades
  • Carved Crests for Champion upgrades
  • Runed Crests for Hero upgrades
  • Gilded Crests for Myth upgrades

Crest sources haven't changed much -- M+ keys, raid bosses, world content, and delves all drop the appropriate tier based on difficulty. The weekly crest cap remains in place, so plan your activities around which crest type you need most. Early in the season, focus on Runed and Gilded Crests since those are what you need for Hero and Myth track upgrades.

Bonus Rolls Are Back

This is a big one. Bonus rolls return in Season 2, giving you an extra shot at loot from raid bosses. Here's how it works:

  • You get 1 bonus roll coin per week
  • Coins are purchased from Orin Straylight starting in Week 8 of the season
  • Using a coin on a boss kill gives you an additional chance at that boss's loot table

This is a targeted gearing tool. If you need a specific trinket or weapon from a raid boss, bonus rolls let you double your chances each week. It won't break the gearing curve, but it smooths out bad luck on key drops. Save your coins for the bosses that drop your best-in-slot pieces -- don't waste them on bosses where you only need minor upgrades.

Ascendant Venomstones: Beyond Myth Track

Want to push past the standard Myth 328 cap? Ascendant Venomstones are special upgrade items that boost select pieces to ilvl 334 (Myth 8/6) or even 337 (Myth 9/6).

These are rare drops from the highest-end content -- think Mythic raid and very high M+ keys. They're the equivalent of last season's "beyond max" upgrade path and exist to give the most dedicated players something to chase after they've capped out their standard gear.

Great Vault Improvements

The Great Vault got a meaningful buff for Season 2. Here's what each raid difficulty now offers:

| Raid Difficulty | Vault Track | Vault Level |

|----------------|-------------|-------------|

| LFR | Champion | 1/6 |

| Normal | Hero | 1/6 |

| Heroic | Myth | 1/6 |

| Mythic | Myth | 6/6 |

This is a nice bump. Normal raiders now get Hero-track vault rewards, and Heroic raiders jump straight to Myth track. It means your weekly vault pick is more impactful even if you're not pushing Mythic difficulty.

M+ vault slots continue to scale with key level, and the third row (world content) scales with the highest delve or world activity completed.

Catalyst Secondary Stat Inheritance

The Revival Catalyst gets a quality-of-life update: secondary stat inheritance. When you convert a piece of gear into its tier set equivalent through the Catalyst, it now inherits the secondary stats from the original item rather than applying a fixed stat distribution.

This matters because it means you have more control over your stat profile when building your tier set. If your best drop has crit/haste and that's what your spec wants, converting it to tier keeps those stats instead of forcing you into a potentially worse combo.

Gearing Priority for Season 2 Launch

Here's the play when the season drops:

  • Run the Coiled Isle campaign for catch-up gear and early Veteran/Champion pieces
  • Start M+ early -- even low keys drop relevant gear in week one
  • Clear Normal raid as soon as it opens for Hero-track vault options
  • Target specific slots with bonus rolls once they unlock in Week 8
  • Funnel crests into your highest-impact upgrades first (weapons, trinkets)

Bottom Line

Season 2 gearing is an evolution, not a revolution. The tracks work the same way, but the ilvl bump, Mistcrests rename, bonus roll return, and Great Vault improvements all make the path to endgame gear feel better.

The biggest win here is the Great Vault change. Normal raiders getting Hero-track vault rewards means you can realistically hit 315 ilvl without ever stepping into Heroic raids, just through vault picks and crest upgrades. That's a solid power level for tackling mid-range M+ keys and getting into Heroic pugs when you're ready.

Plan your first few weeks wisely and you'll be raid-ready faster than you think.

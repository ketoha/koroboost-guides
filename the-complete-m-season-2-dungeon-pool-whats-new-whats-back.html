The Complete M+ Season 2 Dungeon Pool: What's New, What's Back

A guide by Koroboost

Season 2 shakes up the M+ rotation with a brand-new dungeon, returning Midnight favorites, and a couple of legacy picks that'll test your group's adaptability. Here's the full pool breakdown, what left, and what the seasonal rewards look like.

The Season 2 Dungeon Lineup

Eight dungeons total -- five from Midnight and three legacy pulls:

Midnight Dungeons (5)

Altar of Fangs (NEW)

The only completely new dungeon this season. Set in the underground serpent complex beneath the Coiled Isle, Altar of Fangs features venom-themed encounters with environmental hazards like poison pools and constricting corridors. Expect this to be the dungeon everyone's learning in the first few weeks. Tight spaces mean precise pulls matter, and AoE positioning is going to separate clean runs from wipes.

Murder Row

Back from Season 1 and still punishing groups that don't respect the trash. The narrow corridors and patrol-heavy layout make this a routing nightmare at higher keys. If you figured it out last season, the muscle memory carries over. If you didn't, now's the time to learn.

Den of Nalorakk

The troll-themed dungeon returns with its gauntlet-style encounters. Boss mechanics hit hard on Tyrannical weeks, but the trash is manageable if your tank knows the pulls. One of the more straightforward dungeons in the pool.

Blinding Vale

Light-themed dungeon with visibility mechanics that mess with your camera and targeting. Groups that communicate well do fine here. Groups that don't... struggle. The final boss remains one of the tighter DPS checks in the Midnight dungeon lineup.

Voidscar Arena

Arena-style encounters with wave mechanics and add management. This one favors cleave-heavy comps and punishes single-target builds. The boss fights are more about execution than raw throughput, making it a good dungeon for coordinated groups.

Legacy Dungeons (3)

King's Rest (Battle for Azeroth)

A classic returns. King's Rest was one of the harder BfA dungeons, and it's joining the Season 2 rotation. The Golden Serpent, Mchimba, Council of Tribes, and Dazar are all mechanically demanding bosses. If you ran M+ during BfA, you know the deal. If you didn't, study up -- this dungeon punishes ignorance.

Temple of Sethraliss (Battle for Azeroth)

The second BfA pull brings snake-themed encounters that fit perfectly with the Midnight Season 2 venom aesthetic. The healing boss mechanic (Sethraliss herself) is unique in the M+ pool and requires your group to play differently than every other dungeon. Orb running is back, and it's going to cause some heated moments in pugs.

Ruby Life Pools (Dragonflight)

The Dragonflight representative this season. Ruby Life Pools is relatively well-known at this point -- clean mechanics, reasonable trash density, and bosses that reward good movement. It'll likely be one of the more popular keys to push early since most players already know the layout.

What Left the Rotation

Several Season 1 dungeons rotate out, including:

  • Magisters' Terrace -- The Burning Crusade throwback is gone
  • Maisara Caverns -- Leaves after one season

The full rotation keeps things fresh. Love it or hate it, cycling dungeons prevents the pool from going stale and forces players to adapt to new routing and strategies each season. Magisters' Terrace in particular won't be missed by many -- the tight corridors and unforgiving boss mechanics made it one of the more frustrating keys to pug. Maisara Caverns had its fans, but the water mechanics were divisive to say the least.

Seasonal Rewards

Two mounts are up for grabs this season:

  • Keystone Master (KSM): Breath of Blight -- Earned by timing all eight dungeons at +10 or higher within the season
  • Keystone Hero (KSH): Breath of Ruin -- Earned by timing all eight at +15 or higher

Both mounts follow the serpent/venom theme of the season. KSM is achievable for most dedicated M+ players, while KSH requires serious coordination and gear. If you're chasing either mount, start early -- the season timer is real.

Beyond mounts, the M+ rating system continues to reward title upgrades and additional cosmetic unlocks at various rating thresholds. The seasonal affix details haven't been fully revealed yet, but expect the usual Fortified/Tyrannical rotation with a seasonal twist at higher key levels.

Early Season Strategy

Here's how to approach the first few weeks:

  • Learn Altar of Fangs first. It's the new dungeon and everyone's going to be bad at it. Getting ahead of the learning curve gives you a huge advantage in pugs and premades.
  • Brush up on King's Rest and Temple of Sethraliss. If you skipped BfA or didn't push keys during that era, these dungeons have mechanics that will catch you off guard. Watch a video or read a guide before queuing.
  • Don't sleep on Ruby Life Pools. It's familiar, which makes it great for pushing rating early when other dungeons are still chaotic.
  • Route planning matters more than ever. With a mix of tight Midnight dungeons and open legacy layouts, your tank needs multiple routing strategies ready to go.

For detailed boss strategies, trash routing, and comp recommendations for every dungeon in the pool, the Midnight Mythic Plus guide covers it all. And if you're looking to push rating fast or grab KSM without the pug headache, M+ key services can get you there.

What to Expect

Season 2's dungeon pool is well-balanced. You've got mechanically complex legacy dungeons, familiar Midnight returns, and a brand-new dungeon to master. The BfA additions are going to be the wildcard -- players who remember those dungeons have a real edge in the first few weeks.

The mix of BfA nostalgia, Midnight originals, and one Dragonflight dungeon creates a pool that rewards versatility. Groups that can handle both the tight serpent corridors of Altar of Fangs and the wide-open spaces of King's Rest are going to climb rating faster than specialists.

One more thing: don't underestimate the power of running lower keys during the first week. Even +2 through +5 keys drop relevant gear at the season's start, and completing them quickly builds your rating base for pushing higher later. The players who time +15s in week three are the ones who were grinding +7s in week one.

Start planning your routes now. The season waits for nobody.
